ERA 01

Early Era

Before writing, humans communicated through the body, sound and visual marks.

Gestures, facial expressions and vocal calls carried immediate meaning. Cave paintings made information persistent for the first time, while rhythm and drums helped communities transmit recognizable patterns across distance.

02
3,400 BCE → 1400 CE

ERA 02

Writing Era

Writing gave memory to civilization.

Cuneiform, hieroglyphics, alphabets and manuscripts allowed law, trade, religion, science and history to be recorded. Clay tablets gave way to papyrus, parchment, ola leaves and paper — making knowledge increasingly portable.

▰Tabletinscribed records
✎Alphabetcoded language

03
1450 → 1800s

ERA 03

Print Era

One message could suddenly reach thousands.

Movable type and mechanical printing dramatically reduced the cost of reproducing information. Books, newspapers, posters and pamphlets created mass audiences and accelerated education, politics, commerce and science.

▦Pressmechanical copying
▤Newspapermass information
▣Posterpublic messaging
SHIFT1 → MANY
RESULTMass media
04
1830s → 1990s

ERA 04

Electronic Era

Distance stopped controlling the speed of a message.

Telegraphy converted words into electrical pulses. The telephone carried the human voice, radio broadcast sound to entire regions and television added moving images. Satellites then made live global communication practical.

··–Telegraphcoded electricity
☎Telephonevoice at distance
⌁Radiowireless broadcast

05
1990s → NOW

ERA 05

Digital + AI Era

Every medium converged into connected software.

The web, email, mobile phones, social platforms, video calls and cloud services merged text, audio, image and video into digital networks. Artificial intelligence now adds translation, generation, search, recommendation and automated understanding to communication.
