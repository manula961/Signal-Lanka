01 · ANCIENT COMMUNICATION

Ancient Era

Signals become memory.

Before electronic media, communication depended on human senses and physical materials. Voice, gesture and visible signals could carry urgent meaning, while marks, inscriptions and manuscripts allowed information to remain after the original speaker was gone.

Rock and cave imagery demonstrates the power of visual marks as durable cultural traces. Later written traditions moved information onto prepared surfaces, making knowledge easier to preserve, organize and pass between generations.

FROM ROCK TO MANUSCRIPT

Writing makes knowledge portable.

Palm-leaf manuscripts transformed natural material into a compact writing system. Prepared leaves could be inscribed, arranged in sequence and bound together. Sri Lankan examples survive in museum and library collections, preserving religious, literary, medical and other forms of knowledge.

02 · MASS COMMUNICATION

Middle Era

Messages become mass media.

Printing changed the economics of communication: instead of producing one manuscript at a time, mechanical systems could reproduce the same page for many readers. Newspapers, official publications, books and educational material could circulate repeatedly.

Electrical communication changed the relationship between distance and delay. Telegraph systems encoded text as electrical signals, telephones carried voice through circuits, and radio made it possible for one live transmission to reach a large audience.

THE SCALE REVOLUTION

One message. Thousands of receivers.

Print and broadcast solved different parts of the same problem. Printing created many durable physical copies; radio created a shared live signal. Together they helped establish communication systems capable of addressing publics rather than only individuals.

TYPE → PAGE

Typesetting assembles text into a reproducible form. Ink and mechanical pressure then transfer that form onto paper.

TEXT → SIGNAL

Telegraphy represents characters as coded pulses, allowing information to travel electrically rather than waiting for the physical document to arrive.

VOICE → BROADCAST

Radio converts programme audio into a transmitted electromagnetic signal that many receivers can tune into.

03 · NETWORKED COMMUNICATION

Modern Era

Everything becomes networked.

Modern communication converts text, voice, images and video into digital data. Mobile and internet networks can move that data between people, devices and services at near-instant speed, while cloud platforms keep information available across multiple devices.

The major shift is convergence: tools that once required separate media now operate through shared digital infrastructure. AI adds another layer by helping systems generate, translate, summarize, search and reorganize information.

THE NETWORK LAYER

The message becomes data.

A digital message can be copied without changing the original, routed through many networks, stored in multiple locations and presented as text, sound, image or video. The same infrastructure can support private conversations, mass publishing and many-to-many communities.
